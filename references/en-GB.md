# Target locale: British English (`en-GB`)

Use contemporary, audience-appropriate British English. Apply these rules across the whole deliverable; never import US conventions because a source string or library default uses them.

## Language

- Prefer established British spelling: `colour`, `centre`, `licence` as a noun and `license` as a verb, `programme` outside computing, and `travelling`.
- Follow the project's established `-ise` or `-ize` house style consistently. Do not infer US English from `-ize` alone because both can occur in British English.
- Prefer natural British vocabulary where the distinction matters: `postcode`, `mobile`, `CV`, `holiday`, `flat`, and `ground floor`. Avoid conspicuous regional slang unless the brand requires it.
- Use British institutional and educational terms only when they represent the actual concept. Do not relocate facts or invent UK equivalents for foreign institutions.

## Conventions

- Prefer day–month–year in human-facing dates. Avoid ambiguous all-numeric dates; write the month name when context crosses markets.
- Use 24-hour time or the established house style, and format values through the project's `en-GB` locale support.
- Use local address, telephone, currency, quotation, and punctuation conventions only where the underlying market data warrants them.

## Locale isolation check

Search for accidental US forms and conventions, including `color`, `center`, `zip code`, `résumé` used where the product calls it a `CV`, month–day numeric dates, and US-only institutional terms. Some variants are context-dependent; resolve them from meaning and house style rather than blind replacement.
