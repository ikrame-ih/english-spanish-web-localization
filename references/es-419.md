# Target locale: Latin American Spanish (`es-419`)

Use clear, contemporary Spanish intended for a broad Latin American audience. `es-419` is a macroregional localization locale, not a single dialect and not permission to erase meaningful national differences.

## Language

- Prefer vocabulary that is widely understood across Latin America and natural in context. Avoid Spain-only words such as `ordenador`, `móvil`, `coger` in potentially ambiguous contexts, `empadronamiento`, or `vosotros` unless the subject itself is Spain.
- Avoid strongly country-specific vocabulary, voseo, idiom, legal language, and institutional assumptions unless the brief names that market.
- Use `ustedes` for the plural second person. Choose `tú`, `usted`, or neutral constructions from audience and brand voice; maintain agreement consistently.
- Do not manufacture neutrality with awkward definitions, repeated synonyms separated by slashes, or unnatural Anglicisms. If one term is not adequately portable, ask for a country locale or redesign the string with user approval.
- Preserve source-country names and institutions when they are facts. Explain them when necessary; do not replace them with a supposed Latin American equivalent.

## Conventions

- Use correct Spanish punctuation, capitalization, accents, and `ñ`.
- Do not assume one currency, date presentation, address format, unit preference, legal regime, or telephone format for all of Latin America. Use `es-419` locale data where available and retain unambiguous values; request a country locale when market-specific formatting matters.
- Use sentence case for UI unless the house style requires otherwise. Keep CTAs concise, natural, and faithful to the action.

## Locale isolation check

Run a Spainism pass and a country-specificity pass. Reject unexplained `vosotros`, Spain-only administrative assumptions, and narrow national terms presented as pan-regional. Also reject a mixture of forms from different Latin American markets. When the product targets one country, use a country locale such as `es-MX`, `es-AR`, or `es-CO` instead of claiming the copy is universally Latin American.

Reference hierarchy: project termbase and observed product usage; ASALE/RAE pan-Hispanic resources; authoritative sources in representative target markets; Unicode CLDR or the runtime locale library for structured locale data.
