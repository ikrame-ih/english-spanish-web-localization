# Target locale: US English (`en-US`)

Use contemporary, audience-appropriate US English. Apply these rules across the whole deliverable; never import British conventions because a source string or library default uses them.

## Language

- Prefer established US spelling: `color`, `center`, `license` as noun and verb, `program`, `traveling`, and `organize`.
- Prefer natural US vocabulary where the distinction matters: `ZIP code`, `cell phone`, `resume`, `vacation`, `apartment`, and `first floor`. Avoid conspicuous regional slang unless the brand requires it.
- Use US institutional and educational terms only when they represent the actual concept. Do not relocate facts or invent US equivalents for foreign institutions.

## Conventions

- Prefer month–day–year in US-facing prose, but avoid ambiguous all-numeric dates when content may cross markets.
- Use 12-hour time with `a.m.`/`p.m.` or the established house style, and format values through the project's `en-US` locale support.
- Use local address, telephone, currency, quotation, and punctuation conventions only where the underlying market data warrants them.

## Locale isolation check

Search for accidental British forms and conventions, including `colour`, `centre`, `postcode`, `CV` used where the product calls it a `resume`, day–month numeric dates, and UK-only institutional terms. Some variants are context-dependent; resolve them from meaning and house style rather than blind replacement.
